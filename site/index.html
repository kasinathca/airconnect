04 / SOURCES & REPRODUCIBILITY

Research methodology

Data provenance, processing decisions, validation strategy, limitations and sources.

1. Data and provenance

The airport-level input is a subset of India Aviation Stats, a cleaned dataset derived from DGCA workbooks. The application bundles actual annual domestic and international traffic for 2022–2025 and a separately calculated national annual series for 2016–2025.

The secondary location source is an MIT-licensed airport coordinate compilation. Coordinates are not included for every IATA code; unmatched records remain accessible in the table.

2. Analytical workflow

Source CSV→Validation→Airport–year table→ML models→Website

Input is checked for unique airport/year/category keys, nonnegative passenger counts and exact agreement with source national totals. All model outputs are generated before the website is opened.

3. Model design

K-Means

Features: log(1 + domestic) and log(1 + international) passenger movements in 2025; standard scaling; k = 3; random seed 37; 20 initialisations. Evaluated with the Silhouette Score.

Random Forest regression

Features: log(1 + preceding annual domestic traffic) and log(1 + traffic two years earlier). Target: log(1 + domestic movements in the target year). Train target year: 2024. Test year: 2025. Evaluation in original passenger units using MAE, RMSE and R².

The baseline predicts that next year's movements equal the preceding year's movements.

4. Interpretation limits

  • Airport passenger movements count arrivals and departures, not unique travellers.
  • Operational-airport counts, traffic-reporting airports and newly constructed airports are different concepts.
  • Low observed traffic does not independently establish infrastructure underutilisation or cause.
  • Missing international movements do not imply that foreign flights are legally forbidden.
  • This model predicts observed annual volume, not flight route approvals or future airport openings.
  • 2020–2021 national totals reflect the pandemic shock; annual trends should not be interpreted as purely policy-driven.
